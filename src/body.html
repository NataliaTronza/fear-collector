Grammar spotlight · used to

used to + verb = something that was true or happened often in the past, but not now.
I used to sleep with the light on. (Now I don't.)

Negative: I didn't use to like the dark. no -d after didn't

Question: Did you use to have nightmares?

Bonus: be afraid of + noun / -ing
I'm afraid of spiders.
She's afraid of being alone.

Locked in the jar: failure

One book on the shelf is glowing. Tap it to open it.

The Lighthouse Keeper

a novel by Clara Moss · unfinished

You take the glowing book from the shelf. It's handwritten. The story is about a lonely lighthouse keeper, and it's funny and sad at the same time. It's good. Really good. But on page 99, it stops in the middle of a sentence.

Between the pages there's a letter. "Dear me," Clara writes. "I have to stop. What if the book is bad? What if people laugh at me? What if I spend years on it and it's all for nothing? I'll put it on the shelf. Just for now."

The letter is from March 1984. Clara never came back to the book.

At the back, there's another note in old, shaky handwriting: "I'm eighty now. I wasn't really afraid of failing. I was afraid of trying and finding out. Since 1984 I've failed at many things, and I survived all of them. The only thing I really regret is page 100."

Under it, in different ink: "Thank you, Clara. Another one for my collection. C."

The five candles

There are five candles: red, white, green, blue and gold. Light them in the right order, and the wax will melt and give you a key. Light them in the wrong order, and a cold wind blows them all out.

Team rules. The four clues are split between you. Each person opens one clue on their own phone and reads it to the team. Don't show your screen! Fewer than four of you? Someone opens two.

Tap the candles in the order you think is right.
